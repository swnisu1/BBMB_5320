When enzyme isn’t negligible

A worksheet to go with the Enzyme-in-Excess widget. You’ll use the sliders to discover when a Michaelis–Menten fit can’t be trusted, and when you need the quadratic (tight-binding) equation instead.

Open the widget →
The situation. You have a low-Km, low-activity enzyme. To make the time course conveniently short, it’s tempting to just add more enzyme. But when [E] is no longer much smaller than Km, a real fraction of substrate is bound in ES, so [S]free < [S]total — and the standard Michaelis–Menten equation (which assumes [S]free ≈ [S]total) starts to lie. The widget simulates data at several enzyme concentrations, fits each with the MM and the quadratic equation, and plots the fitted Km against the true Km.

Part A — Get oriented (set noise low)

  1. Set the lowest enzyme concentration well below Km. Compare the MM-fitted Km and the quadratic-fitted Km to the true Km. Do both equations agree with the truth here?
  2. Step the “highlight” control up through higher enzyme concentrations and watch the left (v vs. [S]) plot. Does the MM curve still appear to fit the data points well by eye? What happens to the curve as [E] rises?
    Look at the fit quality, not just the Km number.
  3. On the right-hand plot (fitted Km ÷ true Km vs. [E] ÷ Km), describe the two traces. Which equation stays on the true value, and which drifts away?

Part B — Put numbers on it

  1. Find the smallest [E]/Km at which the MM-fitted Km is more than ~10% too high (ratio > 1.1). Record it.
  2. Read the MM-fitted Km (as a multiple of the true Km) at [E]/Km ≈ 1 and again at [E]/Km ≈ 10. Record both.
  3. Across the whole enzyme sweep, how close does the quadratic fit stay to the true Km? Give the range you see.
  4. From your numbers, propose a rule of thumb: how low must [E] be (relative to Km) for a plain MM fit to land within ~5% of the true Km?

Part C — Does it depend on the other knobs?

  1. Change kcat while holding Km fixed. Does the MM Km error at a given [E]/Km change? What does that tell you about what actually drives the bias?
  2. Move the true Km across a wide range (nM to µM), keeping the sweep in the same [E]/Km units. Does the pattern of MM inflation depend on the absolute Km, or only on the ratio [E]/Km?
  3. Raise the noise (CV). At high [E], can you still tell the MM fit is biased? Could noise ever make a biased MM fit look acceptable? What does that imply about trusting a good-looking fit?

Part D — Explain and apply

  1. In one or two sentences, explain why the MM fit returns too high a Km when [E] is not ≪ S. Use the idea that [S]free < [S]total.
  2. A colleague has a 50 nM-Km, low-activity enzyme and plans to run the assay at 500 nM enzyme for speed. Using the widget, predict roughly how wrong their MM-fitted Km will be, and recommend what they should do instead.
  3. Challenge. The apparent Km from an MM fit is often approximated as Km(app) ≈ Km + [E]/2. Test this against the widget at a few enzyme concentrations. Where does it hold, and where does it break down?
Instructor key (answers)
  1. A1. When [E] ≪ Km, both equations return ~the true Km; MM and quadratic agree.
  2. A2. The MM curve keeps fitting the points well by eye (high R²) even as its Km becomes wrong; the curve shifts right / needs more total S to reach half-max.
  3. A3. The MM trace climbs above 1 as [E]/Km rises; the quadratic trace stays flat at ~1.
  4. B4. Roughly [E]/Km ≈ 0.2 gives ~10% inflation (increases steadily from there).
  5. B5. At [E]/Km ≈ 1, MM Km is ~1.5–1.7× true; at [E]/Km ≈ 10, ~6–9× true.
  6. B6. The quadratic fit stays within a few percent of the true Km across the whole sweep (noise-limited).
  7. B7. [E] ≲ Km/10 keeps a plain MM fit within ~5%.
  8. C8. Changing kcat does not change the Km bias — the error is set by the ratio [E]/Km, not by activity.
  9. C9. The inflation depends only on [E]/Km, not on the absolute Km.
  10. C10. The bias persists at high [E]; noise widens the scatter and can make a biased MM fit look “good enough,” which argues for replicates and for using the right model rather than judging by R².
  11. D11. At high [E], the enzyme sequesters a real fraction of the substrate, so the free substrate driving catalysis is less than the total added. More total S is then needed to reach half-maximal rate, which an MM fit reads as a larger Km.
  12. D12. 500 nM / 50 nM = [E]/Km = 10, so the MM Km comes out ~6–9× too high. Fix: lower [E] to ≤ Km/10 (~5 nM) and accept a longer assay, or fit the data with the quadratic equation using the known [E].
  13. D13. Km + [E]/2 works at low-to-moderate [E] but underestimates the true bias at high [E] (e.g. near [E]/Km = 10 the widget shows a larger inflation than the approximation predicts).
