When enzyme isn’t negligible

A low-Km, low-Vmax (low-activity) enzyme tempts you to use high [E] to see any signal. But once [E] is no longer « Km, substrate bound in ES makes [S]free < [S]total — and a Michaelis–Menten fit reports an inflated Km. The quadratic (tight-binding) equation gets it right.

The lesson: a good-looking MM fit (high R2) can still report a badly wrong Km. The error grows with [E]/Km — a rough rule is MM is safe only when [E] ≲ Km/10. The quadratic equation below uses known [E], so [S]free is handled explicitly and the true Km is recovered.
[ES] = ½{ (Et+St+Km) − √[(Et+St+Km)² − 4·Et·St] }  ·  v = kcat·[ES]
