When Michaelis–Menten breaks: enzyme is not ≪ substrate

The Michaelis–Menten equation assumes the substrate you add is the substrate that is free, [S]free ≈ [S]total. That holds only when [E]total ≪ [S] + Km. With a low-Km, slow enzyme it is tempting to add more enzyme so the time course is shorter. Doing that soaks up substrate in the ES complex. Below, simulated initial-rate data are fit with both the Michaelis–Menten equation and the quadratic (Morrison) equation, and the fitted Km is compared with the real one.

Why it is tempting: the time course

The two equations

Michaelis–Menten assumes [S]free = [S]total:

v = Vmax[S]total / (Km + [S]total)

Quadratic (Morrison) conserves both enzyme and substrate, [E]t = [E] + [ES] and [S]t = [S] + [ES], so [ES] solves a quadratic:

v = kcat · { ([E]t + [S]t + Km) − √(([E]t + [S]t + Km)² − 4[E]t[S]t) } / 2

The data here are generated with the quadratic, which is the exact steady-state rate for a given total substrate. The quadratic fit has two free parameters, kcat and Km, and uses the known [E]t. The MM fit has two free parameters, Vmax and Km.

At half-maximal rate, [ES] = [E]t/2 and [S]free = Km, so the substrate you must add is [S]total = Km + [E]t/2. That is roughly what an MM fit reports as Km (the dashed line in the top plot is the ratio this predicts, 1 + [E]t/2Km).

Things to try

  1. Start with Classic MM. Raise [E]total one decade at a time. At what [E]/Km does the MM Km become more than 2-fold wrong?
  2. Try Tempting shortcut. Note how much shorter the time course is. Is the MM fit visibly bad? (Check the residuals by eye, or turn the noise up to 5%.)
  3. In the top plot, read off the [E]t/Km where the MM error first leaves the ±10% band. How does that compare with the dashed prediction?
  4. Try Tight binding and raise the noise. How low a Km can the quadratic still resolve once Km ≪ [E]t? Why does the data then look like a titration?
  5. Tick Replot against true [S]free. Why do the same points now fall on an MM curve with the true Km?
  6. Does fitted Vmax (or kcat) suffer as much as Km? What happens to both fits when the highest [S] is only 1–2 × S½, so the data never reach saturation?
